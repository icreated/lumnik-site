# Gel → Dégel → Mouvement — Positioning Design

## Purpose

Reframe lumnik around a broader business problem than legacy software alone:
an enterprise is frozen whenever its operational knowledge can no longer move
into decisions and action. Legacy ERP systems remain the clearest commercial
entry point, but they are one cause of the freeze rather than the product's
boundary.

## Core narrative

The two sites follow the same three-beat story:

1. **Gel / Freeze** — facts are scattered or locked, business meaning lives in
   people's heads, and elapsed time disappears when systems overwrite state.
2. **Dégel / Thaw** — lumnik recovers facts, makes meaning explicit, and rebuilds
   an observation-relative memory of time without writing to source systems.
3. **Mouvement / Flow** — people can ask grounded questions, detect stalled work,
   and connect modern tools without making fragile sources more entrenched.

The canonical closing signature is **“Defrost the enterprise.”**

## Message hierarchy

The first screen must explain the business outcome before the mechanism. It
should say that lumnik puts trapped operational knowledge back in motion, then
support that promise with four immediate proofs: read-only source access,
grounded answers, controlled deployment, and reversibility.

The explanatory center is **Facts · Meaning · Time**:

- **Facts:** ingest and fuse what source systems store.
- **Meaning:** declare what dirty values and lifecycle states mean.
- **Time:** observe successive states to reveal inactivity and rhythm that the
  source never recorded.

Claims about time remain observation-relative. The sites must not imply that
lumnik knows what happened before its first observation or that it operates in
real time.

## French expression

The French site is the primary commercial surface. Its language should be
plain, concrete, and recognizable to SME leaders, métier managers, IT leaders,
and integrators. It should lead with symptoms—answers trapped across systems,
rules known by one person, quotes or approvals that stop moving—and introduce
technical mechanisms only after the reader understands the outcome.

## English expression

The English site is not a literal translation. It may retain more technical
precision and international vocabulary such as “system of record,” “read-only
substrate,” and “operational knowledge,” while following the same narrative.
Its examples may continue to use North American legacy products.

## Scope

Change the home-page metadata, hero, problem framing, central explanatory
sequence, transitions, manifesto, and calls to action. Preserve the existing
visual language and static architecture. Reduce or demote technical detail
where it interrupts comprehension, but retain verifiable proof and links to
the public documentation and source code.

No product capability, deployment mode, real-time behavior, or production
maturity may be invented. Existing legal and trial pages remain unchanged
unless a home-page link requires correction.

## Verification

- Both documents remain valid, navigable static HTML.
- Every navigation fragment resolves to an element on its page.
- Internal file links and referenced local assets exist.
- The French and English stories align conceptually without becoming mirrored
  translations.
- The canonical signature appears exactly as “Defrost the enterprise.”
- No temporal claim exceeds what observation-relative ingestion can prove.
- A local browser review covers desktop and narrow mobile widths.
